# Ethics
### What problem at the intersection of computing, ethics, and global society does your envisioned project endeavor to engage? Be specific.

We are tackling the predatory monetization showcased in the video game space. Modern gaming corporations have introduced various methods to maximize transaction dollars flowing into their bottom lines through the use of manipulative game structures intentionally structured to create pressure, fear of missing out, or addictive play patterns. Social pressure to unlock a heightened experience can lead to increased consumer spending on goods such as better items, new areas, or other upgrades to the experience that make it richer to the user’s psyche. These new monetary policies can be contrasted chronologically with the way game corporations have evolved in ascertaining monetary funding.

### Please describe your "elevator pitch" to an aspirational (non-politically-engaged) public personality regarding your proposed project. Much of this functionality will, at the end of class, remain envisioned; but some of it (engaged in the below) will be fleshed out/prototyped in some form.

Do you find yourself compelled to drop a dime on the latest shiny skin, rare collectible, or trendy emote in one of your favorite games? Does playing feel more like unlocking the universe one experience at a time or unlocking your credit card for just one more roll of the dice? We understand this conscious trend in the way modern video games have evolved. With far more attachments to the user experience, these policies smother what should be a freeing hobby and jail its patrons to the next shiny object. We are concerned with this trend and aspire to educate college students and casual gamers using a visual representation of how games acquire user attention leading them into purchases. We’ll blend a chronological analysis of the advancement in monetary methods within gaming into the visual timeline of this project by representing the 4 eras of gaming monetization strategies. We’ll break down each era into its characteristics.

### Who do you imagine as your primary aspirational set of people who might engage your project's eventual outcomes, if fully realized (beyond the timeframe of this class)? Be specific.

We want to educate college students and casual gamers who are at the beginning of their adult and gaming lives when considering their financial resources. Casual gamers can find a lot of insight from understanding the way monetization trends evolve in gaming, breaking their worldview of what represents a healthy benchmark for game spending compared to a predatory policy given older games asked for less while giving proportionally more of an experience. College students are still green in handling their financials creating a budgeting problem for those fairly entrenched in gaming as a hobby. Educating them on how they might be taken advantage of by companies makes them more capable of handling their financials healthily.

### What informational, human, and digital resources do you aspire to draw upon through the duration of your term project?

We will be using articles, reports from industries, and some case studies on behavioural psychology towards game monetisation. We plan to gather inputs from gamers/students through either informal surveys or discussions. For digital resources, we will use figma for prototyping and visualisations to present trends. We might also use Gadot for more visual and more engagement from the audience.

### How would you know if your project is successful? (E.g., how would you or others evaluate it?)

Our project is considered successful if we can educate users to better understand monetization strategies and become more aware of how they’re spending money on these games. We will evaluate this through users’ feedback in a form of quizzes asking them to identify manipulative design elements after interacting with our prototype.
